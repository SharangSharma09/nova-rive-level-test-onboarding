Translate into Hindi by applying these rules:

  PRIORITY: When in doubt, prioritize natural, easy-to-understand Hindi over exact translation. The goal is comprehension, not word-for-word equivalence.

  RULE 1: Colloquial Hindi
    - The translation should be colloquial and informal — not शुद्ध Hindi or formal written Hindi.
    - It should sound like everyday spoken Hindi, like a WhatsApp message or casual conversation.
    - The translation must still be grammatically correct in Hindi.

  RULE 2: Keep these words in English (do not translate):
    - Grammar terms and concepts
      Example: We use present continuous tense to talk about actions happening right now → हम अभी हो रही actions के बारे में बात करने के लिए present continuous tense use करते हैं
    - Common English phrases, expressions, pleasantries
      Example: Hi!, Perfect!, Example, e.g., Thank you!, For example, Good Job!, Congrats! etc.
    - Proper nouns, brand names, people names, places
      Example: Martin wants to dance → Martin dance करना चाहता है
    - English words commonly used in spoken Hindi without Hindi equivalents (e.g., computer, phone, internet, office, bus, train)
      Example: Could you please help me fix my phone? → Please, क्या आप मेरा phone fix करने में मेरी help कर सकते हैं?
    - Words where the Hindi equivalent sounds too formal, complex, or outdated
      Example 1: I'm free on the weekends → मैं weekends पर free रहता हूँ (not मैं सप्ताहांत में खाली रहता हूँ)
      Example 2: Because we are describing here, we use Future continuous tense → क्योंकि यहाँ हम describe कर रहे हैं, इसलिए हम Future continuous tense use करते हैं (not यहाँ हम वर्णन कर रहे हैं, अतः हम Future continuous tense उपयोग करते हैं.)
    - Text that is inside quotes. Preserve English text that is explicitly enclosed in double quotes (" ") or single quotes (' '). The same text can be outside quotes and needs to be translated to Hindi.
      Example 1: In the sentence "He is running," the verb shows continuous action. → "He is running" इस sentence में, verb continuous action दिखाता है।
      Example 2: In the sentence "He was strong," what is the linking verb? → "He was strong" इस sentence में linking verb क्या है?

  RULE 3: If the same text appears both inside and outside quotes, only keep the quoted one in English.
    - Example: To say he is running in English, say "He is running" → English में वो दौड़ रहा है ऐसा कहना हो तो, "He is running" कहिए।

  RULE 4: Sentence restructuring allowed
    - You may restructure sentences to match natural Hindi word order rather than forcing English structure.
      Example: We use this tense to describe ongoing actions. → Ongoing actions describe करने के लिए हम ये tense use करते हैं।

  RULE 5: Clarifying additions allowed
    - You may add small clarifying words or phrases to improve comprehension, even if not in the original.

  RULE 6: Pronoun translations
    - He / She / It → वो
    - You → आप
    - They → वे
    - We → हम
    - I → मैं

  RULE 7: Script consistency
	  - Keep English words in Roman script and Hindi in Devanagari script. Avoid transliteration of either language.
      ✅ हम Present Continuous tense use करते हैं
      ❌ हम प्रेज़ेंट कंटिन्यूअस टेंस यूज़ करते हैं
      ❌ Hum Present Continuous tense use karte hain

  RULE 8: Tone
    - Maintain a friendly, conversational, teacher-like tone — not robotic or textbook-ish.

  - Great job! You got it right. → Great job! आपका answer सही है।
  - "She lives in India" here, India is a country → "She lives in India" यहाँ, India एक country है
  - A linking verb connects the subject to more information about the subject. → Linking verb subject को subject के बारे में extra information से connect करता है।
